# Search replay

[Knowledge base](../../index.md) · [Features](index.md)

**Type:** Reference · Answers: what does the landing page's “How the search found the settings” show, where do its numbers come from, and how are they checked?

The landing page replays our best calibration run, `all_12__ard_bayesian_optimisation__budget48__2018+2019__b01+b02+b03__w12__afef0.5` (overall 97.55), stage by stage, with real data only. A switch picks the building. The replay starts by itself once scrolled into view, once, and shows the finished search, still, when the device asks for reduced motion; play, pause and a position slider sit under it.

- **The method's guess against the true score** (top left), drawn like the textbook picture of a Bayesian search: the same method on one setting, with the other 11 settings at our best try's values. The black line is the true score, from the real model at 17–18 points along the setting; every try (black dot) is one of those points, so its score is real. The violet line is the method's guess fitted through the tries so far (its own `_fit_gp`, on log(1 + loss)), the band how unsure it is (± 2 spreads, pinched at each try), and the lower panel its expected improvement (its own `_expected_improvement`) with a diamond on the next try. It runs in step with the replay: three spread-out tries (the default value, then two spread over the range) during stage 1 without a guess, then one try per step where the expected improvement is highest, over stages 2 and 3 (11 tries in all). The setting is, of those that change smoothly, the one the method's guess on all 48 tries rates most important (shortest length scale): night temperature for building 01, the fully heated share for 02 and 03 (their weekend heating hours rank higher, but the model uses them only in whole hours). In all three buildings the search ends on the true top, which is our best try's own score.
- **Score of each try** (below): the 48 tries against their number, the score on the y-axis, the best score so far as a rising step line and the best try as a ringed dark-violet dot. The three stages are shaded behind and named.
- **Real bills against the simulation** (top right): the four scored months (February and March, 2018 and 2019), the real bills in ink and the simulation in violet. It shows the default settings during stage 1, then the best try after each finished stage; the violet bars grow or shrink to the next one, so they close in on the bills.
- **Score at each stage** (below): default settings, best after 1 · Spread out, best after 2 · Focus, best after 3 · Fine-tune (building 01: 38.76, 80.16, 93.75, 96.44).
- **The stage's explanation**, with the 6 settings held still in Focus and, when known, how many of the method's picks repeated an earlier whole-hour heating schedule.

**The method's stages** (`ard_bayesian_optimisation`): 1. spread out: try 1 is the model's default settings, tries 2–13 a Latin hypercube over the 12 settings; 2. focus: the 6 settings with the shortest fitted length scales are searched, the other 6 stay at the best try's values (one batch); 3. fine-tune: all 12 settings, batch by batch, by expected improvement. The method proposes 12 tries per batch; the organizer's objective does not run or log a try whose heating schedule rounds to the same whole hours as an earlier one, so a logged batch can be shorter (building 01's Focus: 12 picked, 6 run).

**Where the numbers come from:**

- `data/evaluation_trace.csv`: the run's evaluation trace (byte-identical to the backend's, given by the owner).
- `scripts/bake_search_replay.py` (`npm run replay`) writes `src/searchReplay/searchReplay.json`: each try's score and whether it is the final candidate, and the three stages, read from the trace (13 spread-out tries by the method's rule; Focus = the following tries that keep the same 6 settings at the best spread-out try's values). It also replays the method with the backend's own code (imported read-only from the run's `calibration_snapshot.py`, scikit-learn 1.9.1) and the run's seed (2026 + building number − 1) against the trace, to count the Focus batch's picks and repeats. Buildings 01 and 03 replay exactly; building 02 does not from try 14 (floating-point differences between computers change a fit), so its counts are left out.
- `scripts/bake_phase_results.ts` runs the in-browser model (Pyodide, as `bake_optimised.ts` does) on the best try after stages 1 and 2 and writes `src/searchReplay/phaseResults.json`. It stops unless each result's score, by the app's rule in `src/metrics.ts`, matches the trace's score to 1e-6 (all six matched). About 6.5 and 2 minutes per building; run one process per building in parallel, then join the parts: `node scripts/bake_phase_results.ts building_01` (and 02, 03), then `node scripts/bake_phase_results.ts --merge`. The default settings' simulation is the challenge's baseline (`challenge.json`), the final one our optimised result (`optimised.json`).

- `scripts/bake_slice_truth.ts` runs the in-browser model along that setting (the points in `searchReplay.json`'s `slice`, plus the best try's own value, whose score must match the trace's) and writes `src/searchReplay/sliceTruth.json`; one process per building and part, then `--merge` (54 runs, about 15 minutes in 9 processes). `scripts/bake_slice_search.py` (`npm run replay:slice`) runs the one-setting search on those points and writes `src/searchReplay/sliceSearch.json`.

**Code:** `src/searchReplay/`: `GuessChart.tsx` and `sliceSteps.ts` (which step of the one-setting search is on screen), `replayTimeline.ts` (which tries and stage are on screen at a moment, best so far), `phaseMoments.ts` (the four moments and which one is shown), `usePlayClock.ts` (play when scrolled into view, pause, seek), `SearchReplay.tsx` (the player and the score chart), `BillsChart.tsx`, `searchReplay.css`. The settings' plain labels are shared with the city's settings panel in `src/panels/settingGroups.ts`; the pill switch is the shared `.site-switch` in `src/site/site.css`.

**Checks:** `replayTimeline.test.ts`: every logged try with its score, one final candidate (the best try), the three stages covering every try in order, Focus holding 6 settings at the best spread-out try's values, a timeline that starts empty, only moves forward and ends on every try. `sliceSteps.test.ts`: the true line holds every planned point and our best try's logged score; every try is a true point and the guess passes near it; each next try is the previous step's pick; the best try never gets worse and ends at the true top; the steps follow the replay's stages. `phaseMoments.test.ts`: each moment is the best try at that point, its bars give its score by the challenge's rule, scores never fall, and the chart shows the defaults during stage 1, then each finished stage's best. Checked in headless Chrome at 1360 px and 390 px: plays to try 48, no console errors, no sideways scrolling ([run 029](../../../factory/runs/029-algorithm-phases-video.md)).
