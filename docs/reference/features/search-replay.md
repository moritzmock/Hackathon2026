# Search replay

[Knowledge base](../../index.md) · [Features](index.md)

**Type:** Reference · Answers: what does the landing page's “How the search found the settings” show, where do its numbers come from, and how are they checked?

The landing page replays our best calibration run, `all_12__ard_bayesian_optimisation__budget48__2018+2019__b01+b02+b03__w12__afef0.5` (overall 97.55), phase by phase, with real data only. An epoch is one of the method's batches: it fits its guess on all tries so far, picks a batch where the expected improvement is highest, and runs it. A switch picks the building; the best score so far is shown next to it. The replay starts by itself once scrolled into view, once, and shows the finished search, still, when the device asks for reduced motion; play, pause and a position slider sit under it.

- **The phases** (top): Initial phase, Focus phase and Full phase, lined up with the chart's phase bands and the building tabs (all share the card's edges; the score labels sit inside the plot; each phase is as wide as its share of the tries); a phase too narrow for its name (Focus, on a phone) shows its number only, the name on hover and for screen readers; the current one is outlined and fills up as its tries come in, and clicking one jumps to its start.
- **The real run**: the 48 tries against their number, the score on the y-axis, the best score so far as a rising step line, the best try as a ringed dark-violet dot, and the phases shaded behind (the current one in pale violet). The epochs (the method's batches) set the replay's pace but are not drawn.

**The method's phases** (`ard_bayesian_optimisation`), named as the owner asked: 1. initial phase: try 1 is the model's default settings, tries 2–13 a Latin hypercube over the 12 settings; 2. focus phase: the 6 settings with the shortest fitted length scales are searched, the other 6 stay at the best try's values (one epoch); 3. full phase: all 12 settings, epoch by epoch. The method proposes 12 tries per epoch; the organizer's objective does not run or log a try whose heating schedule rounds to the same whole hours as an earlier one, so a logged epoch can be shorter (building 01: 13, 6 of 12, 10, 12, 7).

**Where the numbers come from:** `data/evaluation_trace.csv` is the run's evaluation trace (byte-identical to the backend's, given by the owner). `scripts/bake_search_replay.py` (`npm run replay`) writes `src/searchReplay/searchReplay.json`: each try's score and whether it is the final candidate; the phases, from the trace (13 spread-out tries by the method's rule; Focus = the following tries that keep 6 settings at the best spread-out try's values); the epochs, from replaying the method with the backend's own code (imported read-only from the run's `calibration_snapshot.py`, scikit-learn 1.9.1) and the run's seed (2026 + building number − 1) against the trace. Buildings 01 and 03 replay exactly; building 02 does not from try 14 (floating-point differences between computers change a fit), so its full phase is cut into 3 equal epochs. No simulation is run and nothing in `vendor/` is changed.

**Code:** `src/searchReplay/`: `replayTimeline.ts` (the timeline: what is on screen at a moment, best so far, where a phase starts), `usePlayClock.ts` (play when scrolled into view, pause, seek), `PhaseStrip.tsx`, `SearchReplay.tsx` (the player and the chart), `searchReplay.css`. The pill switch is the shared `.site-switch` in `src/site/site.css`.

**Checks:** `replayTimeline.test.ts`: every logged try with its score, one final candidate (the best try); the phases and epochs cover every try in order; Focus holds 6 settings at the best spread-out try's values; the timeline starts empty, pauses only when a phase starts, only moves forward and ends on every try; jumping to a phase lands on its start. Checked in headless Chrome at 1360 px and 390 px: plays to try 48, no console errors, no sideways scrolling ([run 029](../../../factory/runs/029-algorithm-phases-video.md)).
